    Key Principles to Remember

    Timeline of Mastery: There's no "good" or "bad"—only where you are on the timeline. Every child learns language to fluency; music follows the same path.

    Follow Your Feelings: Pay attention to players who make you feel something. Feelings are your brain's way of identifying the best musical models.

    Sound-to-Motor Networks: Musical fluency requires mapping sounds in your head to movements on your instrument—not print to motor.

    Use Tab Wisely: Notation can help, but practice must build sound-to-motor networks. Always hear before you play.

    Anyone Can Play by Ear: If you know you can't carry a tune, you can already discriminate pitch. The instrument handles the rest.
